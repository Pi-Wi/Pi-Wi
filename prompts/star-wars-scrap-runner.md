# Story Prompt: The Blade RHO Carries

## Premise

Write a Star Wars–inspired story set on a scavenged-out desert world — a planet
stripped bare by decades of salvage crews, its dunes littered with the bones of
crashed cruisers, gutted droids, and the skeletal remains of a war nobody
living remembers the start of.

## Protagonist

**RHO** — a scrap-runner. She survives by diving into wreck fields other
crews call unsalvageable, cutting free what's still worth credits, and
staying one step ahead of the settlement's debt collectors. She's fast,
underestimated, and fiercely protective of the little she owns.

Weeks ago, in the gutted hold of a ship far older than anything else in the
boneyard, she pulled a half-broken energy blade from the sand. It doesn't
ignite clean — it gutters, hums wrong, cuts when it wants to and not when she
needs it to. She has no training, no order, no lineage. She's kept it
anyway: strapped to her hip like a tool she hasn't figured out yet, half
convinced it's worth more intact than in pieces.

## The Threat

**THE MARSHAL** — an armored enforcer of planetary law, dispatched not for
Rho, but for the blade. Old, precise, and utterly unhurried, the Marshal has
tracked its signature across the wreck fields for weeks. To the Marshal the
blade isn't scrap — it's unfinished business, a relic that was never meant
to leave containment, and its return is not negotiable.

The Marshal doesn't arrive as a raid. It arrives as an inevitability: a
lone armored figure walking into the settlement at dusk, asking one
question, and waiting for an answer.

## Story Engine — questions to explore

- Does Rho know what the blade *is*, or only that it's dangerous and
  valuable? What does she believe versus what's true?
- Is the Marshal a villain, a soldier following orders, or something closer
  to a guardian who believes the blade endangers anyone untrained who
  carries it — including Rho?
- When the blade finally ignites for Rho — on purpose or by accident, in a
  moment of real danger — what does that ignition cost, prove, or unlock?
- Does Rho surrender the blade, run, fight to keep it, or find a third option
  the Marshal didn't anticipate?
- What does "reclaim" mean by the end — recovery, theft, inheritance, or
  release?

## Tone & Texture

- Sun-bleached, scarcity-driven world-building: everything is repurposed,
  nothing is wasted, nothing is new.
- Contrast the Marshal's clean, deliberate authority against the
  improvised, patched-together life Rho has built.
- Let the blade feel *wrong* before it feels powerful — sputtering,
  unpredictable, closer to a live wire than a weapon, until the story
  earns its first true ignition.
- Keep the scale intimate: one runner, one enforcer, one relic, one
  settlement — not a galaxy-spanning war.

## Deliverable

A short story, scene, or scriptment (author's choice of length and format)
built from this premise, culminating in the confrontation between Rho and
the Marshal over the blade.
